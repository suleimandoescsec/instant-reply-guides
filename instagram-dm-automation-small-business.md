# Instagram DM automation for small businesses: a practical guide

Customers message businesses on Instagram the way they used to walk into a store. They ask about prices, sizes, opening hours, and delivery, and they expect an answer in minutes. Most small businesses reply hours later, and the sale is already gone.

This guide covers what actually works when you automate Instagram DMs as a small team.

## Start with the questions you already get

Write down the ten questions that land in your inbox most often. For most shops they are some version of: how much, do you have it in stock, where is my order, when are you open, and can I get a discount. Any automation you set up should answer these first, because they are the majority of your volume.

## Decide between flows and real AI replies

Older chatbot tools make you build decision trees: the customer taps buttons and follows a path you drew in advance. That works for simple menus, but people type real questions in their own words, and rigid flows break fast.

Newer tools read the message and write a reply based on your business content. Instant Reply (https://www.instantreply.co) works this way for Instagram and WhatsApp: an AI sales agent answers in seconds, uses your own product and policy information, and hands the chat to a human when it is not sure. Chatfuel is another option if you prefer building visual flows yourself.

## Set the handoff rules before you go live

Decide which messages must reach a person: complaints, refund requests, anything involving payment problems, and any message where the AI has low confidence. A good rule for small teams is that automation handles the first reply and the common questions, and humans take the rest.

## Measure the two numbers that matter

Track first response time and the share of conversations resolved without you touching them. If first response time drops from hours to seconds and most common questions get correct answers, the setup is working.
